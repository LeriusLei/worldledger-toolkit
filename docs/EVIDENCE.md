# Evidence and research boundaries

## What is demonstrated here

| Artifact | Unit and interpretation | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Sprinkler | A constructed finite world, balanced seed schedule, seven policies, fixed costs/noise settings | Demonstrates decision-relevant versus nuisance information; no learned model or real-task ranking |
| Hybrid counterexample | Two complete candidates and their four transition/reward pairs | Shows possible non-closure under component mixing; no prevalence estimate |
| Finite-world examples | Enumerated models, exact public-history support and minimax computations | Exact only for the stated finite model family, reward/observation interface and horizon |
| Unit tests | Determinism, malformed-input rejection, denominator handling, trace isolation and ledger integrity controls | Passing tests supports implementation behavior, not empirical generality |
| Demo audit ledger | Synthetic records with known/unknown cost entries | Tests bookkeeping, not scientific success or the adequacy of any stopping rule |

The Sprinkler README figure is freshly generated by the released runner using 400 seeds, nuisance cardinality 16, unit probe accuracy and probe costs 0.05. It displays average net utility without inferential error bars. The balanced schedule is a design choice; these values are illustrative finite-task outcomes. The exact configuration and summary are in [demo_results/sprinkler_summary.json](demo_results/sprinkler_summary.json).

## Historical branches

The original private project explored SBCC/Pinductor integrations, battery simulation, NeuronBench decision tasks, DRHC finite-support protocols, Shared Blind Spot generation and representation design, research-stopping feasibility, and candidate/consumer-contract audits. Their existing stop rules remain authoritative.

Several generation and identifiability gates did not pass. Some paid pilots ended early with unresolved provider usage; missing responses and invalid candidates cannot be removed from denominators or converted into scientific evidence. The finite DRHC controls established properties of their registered synthetic contracts; the broader candidate-generation/decision hypothesis was not established. SBS generated no pool evaluable under its frozen natural-signal gate, and later representation checks did not establish readiness. Research-stopping work produced an engineering audit baseline without the externally executable task evidence needed for its proposed scientific claim.

The later consumer-contract work retained a local natural counterexample and multiple zero-effect controls, while independent task/program coverage and an advantage over simple baselines remained unresolved. That material is not repackaged as a general audit benchmark here. Its raw candidates and third-party execution bundles are excluded.

These historical findings explain the toolkit scope. This public release does not reproduce the complete private research archive or its historical experiments, and summaries here are not substitutes for those underlying records. No conference-readiness, novel-method, generalization or acceptance claim follows from releasing these components.

## Reporting new results

State the task and model unit, source identity, public information, generation/request/response/valid denominators, independent run clusters, horizon, policy scope, cost boundary and missing outcomes. Separate source diversity from behavioral diversity and decision diversity. Compare against the simplest baseline that answers the same decision. Preserve prior negative results when changing a protocol; register the new version before examining its outcomes.
